# Scene / room scenario design

A location is a reusable production asset with stable geography, not a one-off
backdrop. Design it once, then reuse it across every shot that happens there.

## Establish a canonical plate first

- Begin a location with one clean establishing view (the canonical plate). It shows
  the whole room: walls, floor, ceiling line, doors, windows, fixed furniture, and
  the dominant light source.
- Treat that plate as the source of truth for the room. Every other angle is derived
  from it by moving the camera only.

## Lock the topology

Record what must never move between angles or shots:

- fixed architecture: door and window positions, wall corners, stairs, built-ins;
- fixed furniture: desk, counter, shelving, and where each sits against the walls;
- lighting: the direction and color of the key light, time of day, and which
  windows or lamps produce it;
- palette and materials: floor, wall, and surface colors and textures;
- fixed vs movable props: what stays put and what a character may carry or move.

## Coverage grid

- Plan the angles the storyboard actually needs. Cover each camera position and
  screen direction the shots will use.
- Keep at least one wide establishing angle plus the matched angles for the planned
  camera positions (reverse, over-shoulder, side).
- When generating extra angles from the plate, change only the camera. Do not
  redesign furniture, move doors, or relight the room.

## Naming and reuse

- Each angle keeps a stable, readable key so later shots can request the exact view.
- Record which landmark each angle shows (for example: "angle facing the door",
  "reverse showing the window wall") so the right view is chosen for the right shot.
- If a needed view does not exist, generate it from the canonical plate rather than
  inventing a new room.
